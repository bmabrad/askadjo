# Situational Message Library

## Introduction

This is a reference library of 88 example conversation threads across 5 dating coaching categories: OPENING, BUILDING ATTRACTION, HANDLING HER QUESTIONS & OBJECTIONS, ESCALATING, and MANAGING THE DYNAMIC. These threads show real-world examples of how to handle specific dating situations, with annotations explaining why each message works.

Use this library to:

1. Pattern-match against incoming user conversations to identify which situational patterns apply.
2. Calibrate your coaching on message tone, length, pacing, strategy selection, and timing of escalation.
3. Recommend specific language patterns and reasoning from proven examples.
4. Identify gaps where the user's approach differs from effective patterns.

Each thread includes the full message exchange (Him/Her alternating) with "Why This Works" annotations on His messages. These annotations explain the psychological principle, frame, or technique being applied, so you can teach the user not just what to say, but why it works.

## OPENING

### Opening on a Dating App — Curiosity Hook

**Situation:** You matched. Her profile mentions she loves cooking and has travel photos from Japan.

**Exchange:**

**Him:** ok I have to ask
> Why: Open loop. Zero effort, maximum curiosity. She can't not respond.

**Her:** ask what

**Him:** something about your profile caught my eye but I'm not sure you want to hear it
> Why: Withholds the payoff. 'Not sure you want to hear it' makes her chase harder. She's now pursuing information he's holding back.

**Her:** now you have to tell me

**Him:** that ramen photo in Tokyo.. you actually made that or you just took credit for the pic
> Why: Profile-specific so it doesn't feel like a line. Challenges rather than compliments — makes her defend herself, which means she's investing.

**Her:** hahaha I actually made it!! I did a cooking class there

**Him:** alright that's actually impressive. most people just take the photo and pretend
> Why: Earned compliment. He made her prove herself first, then rewarded her. 'Most people pretend' frames her as different from the crowd.

**Her:** I swear it was me 😂 I even burned my hand

**Key Techniques:**
- Open loop
- Withholds the payoff
- Profile-specific so it doesn't feel like a line
- Earned compliment

**Exchange:**

**Her:** so what do you do for work

**Her:** haha ok tell me more

> Why: Short origin story that signals ambition and independence. Then flips it to her — evaluator frame.

**Key Techniques:**
- Gives the category without the details
- Makes the work sound exciting through impact, not title
- Short origin story that signals ambition and independence
